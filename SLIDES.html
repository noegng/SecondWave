01
The observation

A closed-ended vault locks your capital. That is the product, not a bug.

During the whole Investment phase, VaultWithdraw answers tecTOO_SOON. The depositor cannot leave. Full stop.

  • But the risk degrades while the price does not move. AssetsTotal only changes when the broker declares an impairment — and nothing forces them to.
  • A loan past its due date and its grace period leaves the share value perfectly intact. It is invisible from the price.
  • We swept the network: 22 of the 40 largest vaults carry an undeclared defaulted loan. Not ours. In the wild.
02
What we built

Vault shares are an MPT. They transfer. The exit already exists — it just had no tooling.

  • Sell a locked position before the redemption date, through an all-or-nothing atomic swap.
  • An analyst next to every offer — not just "risky", but distress discount versus liquidity discount, with the reason.
  • A durable order book. The seller signs once and keeps living; the buyer commits with a deadline; either side can withdraw in one click, on-chain.

The buyer's only real question is not what price — it is why is the seller cutting it. That is what the rating answers.

04
Major fixes we surfaced

Two things a product cannot work around

XLS-56 · Batch

"Success" does not mean the trade happened

  • Delivery and no-op return the same tesSUCCESS, the same metadata, the same fee. We froze two Devnet transactions side by side: rigorously identical.
  • The per-leg execution report specified by XLS-56 is absent. We rebuild the legs by hand and reconcile balances.
  • 3 of 4 flags hand over the shares without collecting the price.
Dev wallet

The official wallet cannot get you in — or out

  • It sends an XRP deposit as a token object. The vault refuses. The user cannot edit the JSON. Dead end — we fixed it and opened a PR.
  • It knows neither Batch nor Escrow. No wallet can sign an atomic swap, so every peer-to-peer market is forced to be custodial.
05
What would have been great to have

An escrow that does not name its recipient

Today the seller must know the buyer before committing: the buyer's address is inside what the seller signs, and an escrow demands a Destination. So there is no way to say "1,000 shares at this price, to whoever takes them first".

  • The usual objection — anyone could claim it — does not apply here.
  • In a private vault, access is already bounded by a permissioned domain (XLS-80) and its credentials (XLS-70). Everyone who could answer is already trusted.
  • The network re-checks membership at deposit, at transfer, and again at escrow finish — we tested it.

Naming the destination a second time adds no security. It only removes the possibility of a market. Let an escrow point at a domain — and private vaults get a real on-chain order book.

06
The one thing still off-chain

There is no way to ask to join

Everything else lives on the ledger. Onboarding does not.

  • The only transactions that exist are CredentialCreate, CredentialAccept, CredentialDelete. None of them is a request.
  • The issuer pushes the credential; the newcomer can only accept it. An outsider has no on-chain way to raise their hand.
  • So the first step of onboarding happens in a form, an email, a Discord — off-chain, by definition, for a protocol whose selling point is on-chain identity.

Concretely: a buyer who finds an offer on our book and is not a domain member sees "credential missing" — and has no button to press next.
